On January 2, 2026, Alberta brought in the Family Focused Protocol (FFP), which changed how the Court of King's Bench handles family law cases. It replaced the Family Docket Court process in Edmonton and Calgary, which held its final sittings on December 19, 2025. The FFP applies at every judicial centre (a place where the court sits) in Alberta, so families in St. Paul, Vermilion, Cold Lake, Lloydminster, and everywhere else face the same requirements.

The official source is Notice to the Profession and Public NPP#2025-04 on the Alberta Courts website. It was first issued December 10, 2025, and revised August 10, 2026.

## What Is the Family Focused Protocol?

The FFP is a set of mandatory procedures that aim to reduce conflict, encourage early resolution, and keep children's best interests central. The FFP applies to federal *Divorce Act* cases (married spouses seeking divorce, parenting orders, and support) and to provincial *Family Law Act* cases (unmarried parties, guardianship, contact, and parentage).

Before a judge hears your case at a contested hearing (where the parties disagree), you need to have made a genuine effort to resolve things on your own. Going to court should be the necessary next step, not the first one.

## Three Process Streams

Every FFP case goes into one of three streams:

**Regular Family Process.** For contested cases where the parties have completed the mandatory pre-court steps and still need a judge to step in. Most cases go here.

**Desk Process.** For cases that can be resolved without a court appearance. If both parties agree on the terms, certain orders can be processed on the papers alone.

**Urgent Process.** For safety-sensitive or time-critical situations. These include family violence, child safety concerns, a risk of a child being removed from the province, and Emergency Protection Order reviews. If you face immediate safety concerns, you can skip the mandatory pre-court requirements and see a judge quickly.

## The Four Mandatory Pre-Court Requirements

Before you can use the Regular Family Process, you must complete four steps. They are mandatory unless your case is urgent or the court has waived or deferred (excused or postponed) a requirement. A waiver or deferral must be requested by desk application (a written request decided on paper), under the process in Appendix B of the Notice. Without completing the steps or getting a waiver or deferral, your case will not be scheduled for a hearing.

### 1. Parenting After Separation Course

If your case involves children under 18, both parties must complete the free online Parenting After Separation (PAS) course from the Alberta government. It covers how separation affects children, co-parenting, and the court process. The court needs a certificate of completion, dated within the last two years, before your case moves forward.

### 2. Full Financial Disclosure

If your case involves child support, spousal or partner support, or property division, both parties must exchange complete financial disclosure in digital format before the first court appearance. A general summary is not enough. The court expects detailed documents, including tax returns, pay stubs, bank statements, and investment records.

Business owners must also include corporate financials, shareholder agreements, and dividend records. Incomplete disclosure will stall your case. If the other party refuses to produce their financial information, you can file a Notice to Disclose application to make them produce it.

### 3. Alternative Dispute Resolution

Both parties must have tried mediation, collaborative law (lawyers and parties work to settle outside court), or another court-approved alternative dispute resolution (ADR) process within the previous six months. Private mediation can count. The court wants evidence of the attempt. The goal is to agree on as many issues as possible before a contested hearing.

### 4. Family Court Counsellor Meeting

This step applies to self-represented litigants (people without a lawyer) in Edmonton, Calgary, and Red Deer, where Family Court Counsellors are available. If you represent yourself there, you must meet a Family Court Counsellor before your case is scheduled.

## What Happens at the Mandatory Intake Triage Conference

Once the mandatory pre-court requirements are met and a claim is filed, your first court appearance will be a Mandatory Intake Triage (MIT) Conference. This is a one-hour hearing before the MIT Justice assigned to your family. It is held in a courtroom and on the record (recorded by the court).

The MIT Conference is not a trial. The justice assesses your case, decides how it should proceed, and may:

- Grant interim relief, including temporary child support, spousal support, parenting arrangements, or advance costs (early payment toward the other party's legal costs)
- Check for special circumstances, such as domestic violence, security concerns, cultural accommodations, or whether to include a child's views through a judicial interview (the judge talking with the child)
- Set the path toward a Settlement Conference (a judge-led hearing aimed at settlement)

Under the FFP's assigned justice model, the MIT Justice is assigned to your family and oversees all interim needs and case conferences through to the final decision. This gives your case consistent oversight from one judge. The only exception is the Settlement Conference, which normally uses a different justice to preserve neutrality in case there is a trial. The MIT Justice can conduct it only if the parties consent and the Justice agrees.

## Exceptions for Urgent Matters

Protection order applications go through the Urgent Process and are not subject to the pre-court requirements. If you are not safe, do not wait. Contact a lawyer or apply directly to the court.

## What This Means for You

Expect more preparation before your first court appearance than in the past. The court expects more from both parties before it gives your file a judge's time. Still, the FFP is designed to produce better outcomes: parties arrive better prepared, with a clearer view of the issues.
