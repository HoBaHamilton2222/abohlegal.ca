You did the hard part already. You were accepted to a Canadian designated learning institution, got a study permit, and moved your life here to study. Now the real question. How do you turn that investment into permanent residence?

The PR route is not automatic. Rules have tightened since late 2024. But the pathways are real, and if you plan early, they work. Here are the doors open to you in 2026.

## Step One: The Post-Graduation Work Permit

The Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) is an open work permit that lets you work for almost any Canadian employer after you graduate. It is the bridge between your study permit and the Canadian work experience you need for most PR programs.

Rules shifted on November 1, 2024, and they matter. If you applied for your study permit on or after that date and completed a non-degree program (a college diploma or certificate), your program must be in an eligible field of study tied to long-term labour shortages. IRCC uses Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) codes. As of the July 4, 2025 update, 920 codes are eligible. Verify yours on the IRCC site before committing.

Graduates of bachelor's, master's, and doctoral programs at public designated learning institutions are not subject to the CIP field-of-study requirement.

Other PGWP basics that still apply in 2026:

- Your program must be at least 8 months long (900 hours in Quebec).
- You must have studied at a PGWP-eligible DLI.
- Most applicants now need to submit proof of language results.
- You can only receive one PGWP in your lifetime.

PGWP length tracks program length, up to a maximum of three years for programs of two years or more.

One trap: public-private partnership college programs (private colleges delivering a licensed public-college curriculum) have been largely excluded from PGWP eligibility for programs starting after May 15, 2024 (in-province) or February 1, 2023 (cross-province). Verify directly against IRCC before enrolling in any such arrangement.

## Building Canadian Experience Class Work Experience

Once you have your PGWP, the work you do under it can count toward the Canadian Experience Class (CEC) under section 87.1 of the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations*. CEC is the most direct Express Entry pathway for former international students.

The requirement is one year of skilled Canadian work experience, measured as 1,560 paid hours, gained within the three years before you apply. That is full-time at one job (30 hours a week for 12 months), equivalent part-time work, or multiple concurrent jobs totalling the same hours.

The work must be in a National Occupational Classification 2021 TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation. TEER 0 is management. TEER 1 is professional work that usually requires a degree. TEER 2 and 3 cover skilled technical roles and trades. TEER 4 and 5 do not qualify.

Self-employment, volunteer work, and work done while you were a full-time student do not count toward CEC. You also need Canadian Language Benchmark 7 for TEER 0 and 1 occupations, and CLB 5 for TEER 2 and 3.

Plan your PGWP year around hitting 1,560 hours in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 role. If the first job you accept is a TEER 4 retail or service role to pay the bills, those months do not build CEC eligibility. That is a painful lesson to learn at month 11.

## Express Entry in 2026

Express Entry manages applications under CEC, the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Federal Skilled Trades Program, and part of the Provincial Nominee Program. It ranks you by the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) and invites the highest-scoring candidates to apply.

CRS awards points for age, education, language, Canadian work experience, and spouse factors. The core score maxes out at 600. A provincial nomination adds another 600 and is almost always a ticket in.

Draws come in two forms. General draws take candidates above a CRS cut-off across the pool. Category-based draws (introduced May 2023) target specific profiles with lower cut-offs. For 2026, the Minister of Immigration confirmed these categories in February:

- French-language proficiency
- Healthcare and social services
- Science, Technology, Engineering and Math (STEM)
- Trades
- Education
- Transport (new for 2026)
- Physicians with Canadian work experience (new for 2026)
- Senior managers with Canadian work experience (new for 2026)
- Researchers with Canadian work experience (new for 2026)
- Skilled military recruits (new for 2026)

If your occupation fits a category, your path to an invitation is often shorter.

French is the other lever. NCLC 7 or higher in all four French abilities adds 25 or 50 CRS points depending on your English score, on top of access to French-language category draws.

Occupation lists under each category change. Verify your NOC code against the current IRCC category page before you count on a particular draw.

## The Alberta Advantage Immigration Program

If your CRS score is not high enough for a federal draw, a provincial nomination often is. The Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) has several streams. For international students who studied or now work here, four matter most:

**Alberta Opportunity Stream.** For candidates living and working in Alberta with a qualifying job offer. You apply directly to AAIP, not Express Entry. The February 2, 2026 draw invited 915 candidates at a minimum score of 57.

**Alberta Express Entry Stream.** For candidates already in the federal pool who meet Alberta priorities. Dedicated pathways run for Health Care, Accelerated Tech, Law Enforcement, and Priority Sectors including Construction. A nomination adds 600 CRS points.

**Rural Renewal Stream.** For candidates with a job offer in a designated rural community, supported by a community endorsement letter. The February 10, 2026 draw invited 212 candidates at a minimum score of 54. Several St. Paul and Lakeland-region communities participate.

**Tourism and Hospitality Stream.** For workers at eligible Alberta tourism-sector employers who meet language and experience criteria.

AAIP also runs Graduate Entrepreneur and Farm streams. Eligibility and draw parameters change often, so confirm current requirements at alberta.ca/alberta-advantage-immigration-program before building a plan around any one stream.

## Common Mistakes That Cost People Years

**Changing programs without checking PGWP impact.** Switching into a program that does not meet the CIP field-of-study rule, or into one at a non-PGWP-eligible institution, can eliminate your PGWP entirely.

**Working more than 24 hours off campus.** In 2026, the off-campus limit is 24 hours per week while classes are in session, with unlimited hours during scheduled breaks (capped at 180 days per calendar year). Going over the cap breaches your study permit conditions and can cost you PGWP eligibility.

**Filing CEC work in the wrong TEER.** Misclassifying your job as TEER 3 when it is actually TEER 4 will sink an application. Keep detailed job descriptions and ask your employer for a reference letter that maps your duties to the NOC you are claiming.

**Letting status lapse.** If your permit expires before you apply to extend or transition, you may need to restore status. Mark every deadline and file early.

**Missing the PAL requirement.** For the 2026 cap year, most study permit applicants still need a provincial attestation letter or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL) issued between January 1, 2026 and December 31, 2026. Master's and doctoral students at public DLIs are exempt as of January 1, 2026.

## Plan Backwards from PR

The strongest PR applications start at the DLI-selection stage, not at graduation. Before you enroll, ask three questions. Is the DLI PGWP-eligible? Is the program at least two years long (which gets you a three-year PGWP and more runway to hit 1,560 CEC hours)? If it is a non-degree program, is the CIP code on the current eligible field-of-study list?

Then plan the PGWP year. Target a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 role from the start. If your occupation fits a 2026 Express Entry category, you have a shorter path. If French is a realistic option, invest in it now. If your CRS score will land below the federal cut-off, pursue AAIP in parallel.

Immigration rules are moving fast. Check IRCC and alberta.ca directly before major decisions. Do not rely on advice from people who went through the system even two years ago.
